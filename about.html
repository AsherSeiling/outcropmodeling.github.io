This project uses UAV-based photogrammetry to build high-resolution 3D models of large roadcut exposures in Tennessee. Many parts of these exposures are too tall, steep, unstable, or close to highway traffic to examine safely. A drone can capture those inaccessible portions so the outcrop can be studied in detail on a computer.

We modeled six roadcuts spanning Upper Devonian to Lower Pennsylvanian strata across Tennessee's Eastern Highland Rim and Cumberland Plateau. Overlapping UAV imagery was processed with OpenDroneMap on Tennessee Tech's high-performance computing system, then cleaned and interpreted in CloudCompare and Blender to produce georeferenced digital outcrop models (DOMs).

The poster focuses on a paired roadcut near Celina, Tennessee, where opposite sides of the highway expose the Lower Mississippian Fort Payne Formation and a bioherm whose geometry changes across the exposure. The models let us correlate marker horizons between the two faces and begin reconstructing the 3D architecture of the bioherm across the highway gap.
